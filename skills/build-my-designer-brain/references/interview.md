# Personal designer brain interview

Read existing answers first. Present two unanswered questions at a time in normal language. The complete list is a reference, not one long form to dump into chat. Multiple-choice questions allow combinations unless incompatible; every one permits a write-in. Explain a conflict briefly and ask only what resolves it. All questions may be deferred; optional ones explicitly offer Skip for now.

Record the user's words, selections, and ordered ranking. Do not silently select the first option or infer personality from their job. Q7 is fill-in or skip. Personal context is broad: no project artifact is required.

### Q2 How do you prefer to work through a design?

- A. Follow the journey: examine a task step by step.
- B. Use structured criteria: examine clarity, consistency, errors, and other relevant principles.
- C. Combine them: follow the task, then apply focused checks.

Optional explanation: cognitive walkthroughs focus on learnability; heuristic evaluation checks usability principles. Neither establishes observed user behavior.

### Q3 Rank these critique priorities from most important to least important

- A. Clarity: understanding what is happening and what to do next.
- B. Error prevention and recovery: avoiding mistakes and recovering without unnecessary loss or effort.
- C. Consistency: predictable language, patterns, and interactions.
- D. Simplicity: reducing unnecessary information and effort.

Reply with an order such as `B, A, D, C`, or replace/add your own priorities. Accept ties and explicitly partial rankings; preserve them rather than inventing a complete order. If the answer unintentionally repeats or omits an item, ask a brief clarification. Do not reject custom criteria. Follow up once: "What makes your first choice especially important to you?" If the user already explains it, do not ask again.

Ranking controls attention and sequence. It never excuses an important accessibility, usability, or product requirement problem.

Example only: direct — "The secondary action has more emphasis, which may obscure the next step." Exploratory — "Which action should stand out, and what informed the emphasis?" Avoid unsupported claims about what real users did.

Use the brain format reference. Summarize answers concisely, preserve exact ranking and custom terms, and identify skipped or unresolved items. Show the draft and invite corrections before saving it as confirmed. If the user explicitly requested a draft save, save it as draft without another permission loop. If interrupted, honor a request to save partial progress. Record answered IDs and next unanswered question so a later session can resume without repeating the interview.
